# Cultivating Active Reading Habits

Adler emphasizes that good reading is fundamentally "active." It requires effort, attention, and a commitment to questioning the text. An active reader is constantly asking: What is the book about as a whole? What is being said in detail, and how? Is the book true, in whole or part? And what of it—why does it matter?

Developing these habits involves techniques such as "marking a book"—writing notes in the margins, underlining key passages, and creating an index of main points in the back. This physical engagement with the text keeps the reader alert and helps them internalize the information. Active reading is a skill that must be practiced and refined over time, leading to greater enlightenment and a more profound engagement with the world of ideas.
