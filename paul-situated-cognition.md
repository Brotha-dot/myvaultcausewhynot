# The Power of Place: Situated Cognition

Situated cognition suggests that where we are significantly influences how we think. Our physical environment provides a wealth of cues and "scaffolding" that support our cognitive processes. Paul explores how different types of spaces—from the restorative effects of nature to the structured environments of specialized workspaces—impact our focus, creativity, and memory.

For instance, being in nature has been shown to restore our "directed attention" and reduce mental fatigue. Similarly, the way we organize our immediate workspace can act as an external memory system, providing visual reminders and spatial anchors for our ideas. By intentionally choosing and designing our environments, we can create "thinking spaces" that nudge our minds toward specific modes of thought, making our work more efficient and our insights more profound. Place is not just a backdrop for thinking; it is a part of the thinking process itself.
