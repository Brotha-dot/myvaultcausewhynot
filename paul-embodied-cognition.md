# Thinking with the Body: Embodied Cognition

Embodied cognition is the idea that our physical movements, sensations, and internal bodily states are integral to our thinking processes. Annie Murphy Paul explains that our brains are constantly monitoring our bodies for "data" that helps us make decisions and understand the world. For example, "interoception"—the ability to perceive internal bodily signals like heart rate or muscle tension—is closely linked to emotional intelligence and intuitive decision-making.

By becoming more aware of these bodily signals, we can improve our cognitive performance. Paul also discusses the power of "gesturing" as a way to think. When we use our hands to explain a concept, we are not just communicating; we are using physical movement to help our brains process information and find new ways to structure our thoughts. Thinking with the body allows us to tap into a rich source of non-verbal intelligence that is often overlooked in traditional cognitive models.
