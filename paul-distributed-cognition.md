# Thinking with Others: Distributed Cognition

Distributed cognition is the idea that thinking is often a collective process that happens between people and their tools. Paul argues that we are "social thinkers" by nature, and our intelligence is significantly boosted when we collaborate and share information with others. Social interaction provides opportunities for "social memory loops," where ideas are refined and reinforced through dialogue and feedback.

Collaborative problem-solving allows us to distribute the cognitive load across multiple minds, leveraging the diverse perspectives and expertise of the group. Furthermore, teaching others or explaining concepts to a peer forces us to clarify our own understanding and identify gaps in our knowledge. By recognizing that our minds are part of a larger social network, we can move beyond the myth of the "lone genius" and embrace the power of collective intelligence. Thinking with others is not a sign of weakness; it is a fundamental human cognitive strategy.
