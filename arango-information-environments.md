# Notes as Information Environments

In Duly Noted, Jorge Arango argues that our personal notes should be viewed as "information environments" rather than mere collections of files. Just as physical architecture shapes our behavior and interactions within a building, the structure and design of our note-taking systems shape our cognitive processes. An information environment is a space where information is organized to support specific tasks, such as learning, planning, or creating.

When we treat notes as environments, we prioritize wayfinding and structural consistency. We ask how easy it is to move between ideas and whether the "architecture" of our system provides enough context to make sense of information over time. This perspective shifts the focus from simple capture to the intentional design of digital spaces that foster deep work and long-term knowledge retention.
