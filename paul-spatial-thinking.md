# Thinking with Space and Movement

Paul emphasizes that spatial thinking is one of our most powerful cognitive abilities. Our brains evolved to navigate a physical world, and we can leverage this evolutionary heritage by "spatializing" abstract information. This involves using tools like mind maps, concept diagrams, and physical layouts to visualize and manipulate ideas in space.

Movement also plays a crucial role in extension. Walking, for example, has been shown to increase creative output and problem-solving ability. The rhythmic movement of the body seems to loosen the constraints of focused attention, allowing for more associative and expansive thinking. By incorporating spatial visualization and physical movement into our daily routines, we can unlock new levels of cognitive flexibility and insight. Our minds are at their best when they are moving through and interacting with a rich, three-dimensional world.
