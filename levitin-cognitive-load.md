# Managing Cognitive Load in the Information Age

Levitin argues that the human brain was not designed for the constant stream of data we face today. Our "attentional filter" is easily overwhelmed, leading to a state of chronic cognitive load where we struggle to prioritize and process important information. This overload results in forgetfulness, stress, and a decrease in productivity. To manage this, we must understand the limits of our biological systems and learn to selectively attend to what truly matters.

One key strategy is to minimize "attentional switching costs"—the mental energy lost when jumping between tasks. Each switch requires the brain to reconfigure itself, which consumes glucose and leads to fatigue. By batching tasks and creating environments that minimize distractions, we can protect our limited cognitive resources and maintain higher levels of performance throughout the day. Organizing our lives is not just about being tidy; it's about biological efficiency.
