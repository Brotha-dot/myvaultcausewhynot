# Overcoming Decision Fatigue through System Design

Every decision we make, no matter how small, consumes mental energy. Over the course of a day, these decisions add up, leading to "decision fatigue"—a state where our ability to make good choices is significantly impaired. Levitin suggests that we can overcome this by designing systems that automate or eliminate low-value decisions. This might mean setting a standard routine, pre-planning meals, or simplifying our wardrobe.

By reducing the number of choices we have to make, we preserve our "decision-making capital" for the things that truly matter. System design is about creating a "choice architecture" that nudges us toward better outcomes with less effort. When our environment is organized and our processes are streamlined, we experience less stress and have more energy for the creative and strategic work that defines our most productive moments.
